    The kit program

    The Defense Logistics Agency buys special operational equipment (tactical gear, diving equipment, medical kits and thousands of other items) through a few long-term contracts. The prime contractors don't make most of it. They find it, bundle it into kits and ship it, buying from a network of smaller suppliers, which makes it a sales-and-logistics business as much as a manufacturing one. Nationwide figures.

      About the data

      Source. Every number comes from USAspending.gov, the federal government's official record of contract spending. "Hampton Roads" means work performed in Chesapeake, Hampton, Newport News, Norfolk, Poquoson, Portsmouth, Suffolk, Virginia Beach, Williamsburg and the counties of Gloucester, Isle of Wight, James City, Mathews and York.

      Obligated, not paid. An obligation is a legal promise to pay. It's the standard measure of contract spending, but money is paid out over time, sometimes years later.

      The 90-day delay. The Defense Department publishes contract actions about 90 days after they're signed, so the newest months look smaller than they will be, and a contract that seems to be ending may already have been extended. Months that are still filling in are marked.

      Final end date. Many contracts have options the government can exercise to extend them. The runway uses the last date a contract could run with every option exercised, read from each contract's own page. Until that page has been read, the current end date is used.

      Contracts of $1 million and up are tracked one by one (the threshold is a setting). Smaller orders, of which there are many thousands, still count in every total and breakdown.

      Companies are grouped by parent company when USAspending lists one, and by name otherwise. This is an educational project and not an official source for bid decisions.
